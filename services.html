Illustrative scenario

When customers don’t understand the value.

Imagine a restaurant that sells a premium dish for ₹700 and has a Google rating of 3.14. Some potential customers may question whether the dish is worth the price. Others may be discouraged by negative reviews. These are two separate challenges — not a single “redesign” answer.

Scenario image — plated dish or menu on a phone
pictures/services/05-scenario.jpg

Challenge 01

Unclear product value

Customers see the price but don’t understand what makes the dish different.

Possible recommendations

  • Improve menu descriptions.
  • Explain ingredients and preparation where relevant.
  • Clarify portion sizes and what is included.
  • Use accurate, high-quality food photography.
  • Improve the mobile menu experience.
  • Make important product information easier to find.

Before

Signature Grilled Fish — ₹700

After

Signature Grilled Fish — ₹700
Freshly prepared fish, marinated in our house spice blend, grilled to order, and served with seasonal sides.

Only use this kind of copy if it accurately describes the actual product.

Challenge 02

Customer trust

A clearer menu cannot fix poor service or inconsistent quality.

Possible recommendations

  • Review available customer feedback.
  • Identify recurring complaints.
  • Discuss the findings with the owner.
  • Prioritise issues the business can address.
  • Improve customer communication where needed.
  • Recommend operational changes when appropriate.
  • Track genuine customer feedback over time.

Potential solution

Better product communication, clearer customer information, and a practical improvement plan based on the actual problems identified.

Intended outcome

Help customers make more informed decisions and give the business a clearer path toward improving its customer experience.
